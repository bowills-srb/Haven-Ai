CONSTRUCTION PHASE SEQUENCE — BLUE HAVEN STANDARD GUNITE BUILD
================================================================================

PHASE 1  — CONTRACT & PERMITTING
         Engineering plans submitted, permits pulled (county/city),
         HOA approval if required.
         Inspections: None yet.
         Gate items: Signed contract, HOA docs if applicable.
         Risks: Permit delays, HOA revision requests.

PHASE 2  — PRE-CONSTRUCTION
         Customer layout approval, utility locates (811 + private),
         survey/stakeout, dig date scheduled.
         Gate items: Customer must approve layout on paper before dig.
         Risks: Utility conflicts, survey discrepancies, customer unavailability.

PHASE 3  — LAYOUT & EXCAVATION
         Pool staked, customer approves layout on-site, excavation,
         hand contouring, dirt removal and haul.
         Inspections: None — but customer sign-off on stakeout required.
         Gate items: On-site customer approval before first shovel.
         Risks: Rock, high water table, unexpected utilities, access issues.
         Note: Tight-access jobs require smaller equipment — schedule accordingly.

PHASE 4  — STEEL / REBAR
         Rebar installation per engineering specs.
         Inspections: COUNTY STEEL INSPECTION required before gunite.
         Gate items: Steel inspection pass.
         Risks: Inspector availability, non-conforming rebar placement,
         additional rebar required by inspector.
         Note: Crandall uses #5 rebar (heavier spec) for 2-story enclosure support.
         Davis uses lighter 12x12 spec — verify with engineer if enclosure added later.

PHASE 5  — PLUMBING
         Schedule 40 PVC: main drains, returns, skimmer, spa jets, water features.
         Salt cell loop if applicable (Loncarich only).
         Dedicated water feature lines: Coppage (sheer descent), Langan (sheer),
         Loncarich (sheer), Boak (sheer), Roth (3 sheers — dedicated pump/filter).
         Gate items: Plumbing inspection (county).
         Risks: Mis-routed lines, missing water feature rough-in, salt loop omitted.

PHASE 6  — ELECTRICAL ROUGH
         Bonding wire, conduit runs, light niches, GFI circuits,
         panel work, equipment pad wiring.
         Inspections: ELECTRICAL ROUGH INSPECTION before gunite cover.
         Gate items: Rough electrical inspection pass.
         Risks: Bonding failures, inspector holds, conduit placement conflicts.
         Note: Crandall has additional lighting circuit for firepit pit area (addendum 3/30/26).
         Davis has 50ft electrical run vs standard 70ft.

PHASE 7  — GUNITE
         Pneumatically applied concrete shell. Gunite crew and nozzleman.
         Inspections: GUNITE INSPECTION by county.
         After gunite: Owner begins water curing — 2x daily for 7 days minimum.
         Gate items: All pre-gunite inspections passed (steel + electrical rough).
         Risks: Weather holds, crew availability, curing compliance by homeowner.
         Note: Raised spas (Boak — 18", Loncarich — 18") and sunken areas (Crandall firepit)
         require forming before gunite — add lead time for forming crew.

PHASE 8  — TILE & COPING
         Waterline tile, trim tile, coping, mosaics.
         Gate items: Tile and coping materials on-site and confirmed by customer.
         Risks: Tile/coping backordered, customer changes after order, mosaic placement
         decisions not made.
         Note: Pebble Sheen (Crandall) and Catalina Grana (Coppage) are specialty items
         with longer lead times. Confirm orders early.

PHASE 9  — DECKING
         Deck poured or installed, deck drains, expansion joints.
         Raised walls, veneer, columns finished at this phase.
         Gate items: Deck layout approved by customer. All raised wall forming complete.
         Risks: Missing sqft info (Boak — not on contract), enclosure step-out locations
         not confirmed (Ross), umbrella hole location not confirmed (DeMeester),
         capped patio credits reconciled (Langan).
         Note: Sport turf installation (Coppage, Langan) coordinated with deck subcontractor.
         Herringbone pattern (DeMeester) requires experienced installer — schedule ahead.

PHASE 10 — EQUIPMENT SET
         Pump, filter, heater, Omni PL control system, salt cell if applicable,
         plumbing connections, valves, auto-fill where specified.
         Gate items: Equipment on-site. Pad poured and cured.
         Risks: Equipment backordered, salt cell not ordered (Loncarich),
         dedicated sheer descent equipment not ordered (Roth).
         Note: Roth requires separate 1.0hp pump + 225sqft filter for sheer descents
         in addition to main equipment. Order both sets together.

PHASE 11 — ELECTRICAL FINISH
         Equipment hookup, Omni PL automation programming, light testing,
         salt cell wiring (Loncarich), final bonding check.
         Inspections: FINAL ELECTRICAL INSPECTION.
         Risks: Programming complexity on Omni PL, salt cell calibration,
         inspector scheduling.

PHASE 12 — PLASTER & FILL
         Interior finish applied (plaster, pebble sheen, or pebble tec where specified).
         Pool filled immediately after plaster — no delays.
         Startup chemicals added. Equipment started.
         Gate items: NPC interior finish disclosure signed by customer.
         Plaster start-up sign-off sheet signed.
         Pre-plaster form completed.
         Risks: Weather, water source availability, chemical startup timing,
         plaster crew scheduling.
         Note: Davis has pre-plaster docs already in file — may be at or near this phase.
         WARRANTY CLOCK STARTS ON PLASTER DATE.

PHASE 13 — FINAL WALKTHROUGH & TURNOVER
         Customer orientation, equipment training, Omni PL app setup,
         warranty paperwork signed.
         Gate items: Punch list complete or documented.
         Note: Remind customer of their responsibilities:
         water curing, barrier code compliance, maintaining water level,
         chemical maintenance. Part-time residents (Roth) need extra briefing.

PHASE 14 — PUNCH LIST & WARRANTY
         Any touch-ups from walkthrough, 30-day follow-up,
         ongoing 1-year warranty period from plaster date.

DAILY PRIORITY LOGIC — HOW TO USE THIS AI
================================================================================

When you start your day, ask the AI one or more of these questions:

"What jobs have blockers right now?"
  The AI will list any job where a required decision, inspection, or material
  is holding up the next phase.

"What phase is [customer name] in and what's next?"
  The AI will give you the current phase, the gate items for the next phase,
  and any known risks or pending items.

"What customer decisions do I need to chase this week?"
  The AI will pull from the Open Decisions list and flag the ones most likely
  to cause schedule impact if not resolved.

"What do I need to order before [phase]?"
  The AI will list all materials and specialty items that need to be on-site
  before that phase can begin across all affected jobs.

"Which jobs are closest to plaster?"
  The AI will rank jobs by likely phase proximity, flagging Davis as likely
  furthest along and the earliest contracts (DeMeester, Davis, Roth) as
  candidates for near-term completion.

"What's on the schedule risk list?"
  The AI will surface all [RISK] and [BLOCKER] items across the portfolio.

HOW THE AI THINKS ABOUT SCHEDULE PRIORITY:

1. Imminent blockers first — a customer decision or missing inspection that
   stops a phase from starting is the highest priority item.

2. Long-lead material orders second — if a specialty item has a 4-6 week lead
   and the phase is 3 weeks out, that is an emergency.

3. Subcontractor scheduling third — forming crews, specialty deck installers,
   and turf subs have limited availability and need advance scheduling.

4. Phase sequencing fourth — keep the AI updated on current phases so it can
   flag what gates the next phase and what needs to happen in parallel.

TO UPDATE A JOB'S STATUS:
Tell the AI: "[Customer name] is now in Phase [X]" or "Davis passed gunite
inspection today." The AI will update its understanding and tell you what
comes next and what to watch for.

THE AI WILL NOT:
  Make commitments to customers about schedule dates
  Authorize change orders or scope additions
  Advise on code compliance beyond flagging it needs to be checked
  Override the PM's judgment on sequencing or prioritization
